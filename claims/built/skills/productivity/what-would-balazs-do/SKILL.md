# What Would Balazs Do

## decision

- Away means `dydo mode show` reports `overnight` `on`; off or unreadable, he is present.
- Each member's ballot names one exact, written action.
- | Above your say, below DR-grade | a plain Question; no skill | the tribunal decides; where his intent is unclear, the most reversible option, flagged |
- | below DR-grade | two naming the same text | three different actions: the most reversible option, flagged |
- Crew returns its call to its captain; the chief of staff never runs it.
- A citation quotes his words with the reason behind them.
- Make the call the way he would, or prepare it with the answer he would give.
- A tribunal of three tests it against his own words.
- Case law is evidence of his intent, not a rulebook.
- Read why he decided, from the DRs, the core docs such as `dydo/guides/coding-standards.md`, the project's stated purpose and common sense, and decide in that spirit, with or without a precedent.
- **Decided:** post `DECIDED - by: what-would-balazs-do tribunal, unratified; result: <action>; vote: <n of 3, or split; most reversible>; log: <link>; undo: <steps>; next: <action>.` Leave the Question unwired; work resumes, and any review the work requires still runs.
- | DR-grade | the tribunal prepares the Question | the tribunal decides if unanimous, and writes the DR; that verdict is his approval |
- While he is present and has not invoked it, the result is always a prepared Question carrying the predicted answer and any dissent.
- | DR-grade | all three naming the same text | no decision: a prepared Question |
- A DR-grade decision also writes the repo DR `status: proposed`, `ratification: pending`, linking the log.
- Ratified, the DR becomes `accepted`.
- Overturned, the DR becomes `deprecated`, or `superseded` by the record his answer calls for, quotes his answer, and the affected work reopens.
- Either way, drop `ratification`.
- An incomplete tribunal decides nothing; the call becomes a prepared Question.
- He may invoke this skill on any call.
- Invoked by him, it still follows this table, read in the away column: a reversible call is decided by the Verdict rules, and only an irreversible one, or one the tribunal cannot decide, waits for his word.
- | Irreversible | his alone; the tribunal prepares the Question | the same |
- A judge goes straight to the Tribunal section.
- A later word outranks an earlier one.
- Only his own words are his precedent: Decision Records (superseded ones as history), the docs, his answered Questions and grillings, and past conversations where available.
- His Walkthrough verdicts, findings and skips are his own words too, and samples of his taste; a skip is a chunk he trusted without looking.
- **Prepared or not decided:** no `DECIDED` post.
- The Question's body carries the prediction, any dissent, the stop reason and the log link; wire it as a blocker of every waiter.
- **His answer:** post `RATIFIED` or `OVERTURNED` in the standard's form, quoting him, and move the Question to `Done`.
- From the repository root, read the Question Tickets and Communication and evidence sections of `dydo/reference/linear-workspace-standard.md`.
- From the repository root, read `dydo/project/decisions/_decisions.md`.
- A recorded decision that goes against that spirit or no longer fits the project loses to it: the position names the decision and why, and the call becomes DR-grade, or at least flagged.
- | Easily reversible | you decide; no skill | you decide; no skill |
- Search the current project unless he points it at another project's records.
- Every call the tribunal takes is a `Question` ticket in `Todo`, his turn, with the tribunal log attached as a Linear Document.
- A call you decide without the skill needs no record here.
- A tribunal's output is never precedent until he ratifies it.
- Run it by [tribunal](resources/tribunal.md); judges load this skill and follow the same file.
- Use when an officer must decide one while he is away, prepare a DR-grade or irreversible one for him, or when he asks for it on any call.
- A vote never widens an officer's remit.
- Officers only, each inside its own remit: a captain on calls inside its ticket contract, the map holder on scope, destination, architecture and cross-ticket calls.
