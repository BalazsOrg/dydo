Does the ticket captain's review loop feel right?

A clickable model of what happens to a change between "the writer has a candidate" and "it is merged".

The question. When a captain's change is finished, it goes to review. A reviewer can split the review into two seats, one Claude and one Codex. Each seat comes back with findings, and the reviewer combines them into one verdict, which is FAIL if either seat found a blocker. A FAIL becomes a Fix ticket. The fix gets its own commit, and a fresh reviewer looks at it in a new round. Shoulds become tickets of their own, and each nit gets one line with a ruling. A PASS names the exact commit it judged, and only that commit may be merged. After that come the pull request, CI and the merge. Do these rules hold up when the awkward cases arrive: a split verdict, failure after failure, a merge without a PASS on the head?

What this models. The rules as written today in ticket-captain/resources/merge.md, review.md, dydo/reference/linear-workspace-standard.md and statechart/captain/register.md. Two of today's rules differ from the brief this demo was built from: (1) the fifth-FAIL stop is gone (register RM10). A third FAIL now leads to a checkpoint, where the captain writes "one more round, because …" or "escalated: …". (2) The Fix goes back to the writer who wrote the change by default. A fresh writer takes it only when the original writer's context is full or a blocker says the approach was wrong. The demo follows today's rules, and the "Five FAILs in a row" tab shows the difference.

One assumption. The rules call for a checkpoint "after a third FAIL", but they don't say whether the fourth and fifth FAILs need one too. Here, every FAIL from the third onward stops at a checkpoint.
