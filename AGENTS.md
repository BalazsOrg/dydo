# DynaDocs

Linear owns live work. Git and dydo own durable knowledge and evidence.
[dydo/index.md](dydo/index.md) is the root of the documentation.

## Sort yourself

- Opened or spawned with a role? Keep it: an officer holds its Project, ticket or board to the end; crew does its one job and returns.
- Thinking with the user? Load `co-thinker`.
- Load any other skill when the work needs it; each skill names the next.

The user hands out the officer roles by name: a ticket-captain by default, a coordinator only when invoked.

## How work happens here

Work here is organised into tickets, and one agent holds one ticket. You own its outcome: it is done when its contract is met. No ticket? The user dispatched you directly; follow their instructions until one comes.

The process is mechanical: your skills and their resources define it, and you follow them to the letter, without improvising. The work is not; it takes all your intelligence.

The Project's spec is the source of truth. It outlives every ticket and goes only when the Project is done; your ticket's contract defines only your outcome. Know how your ticket fits the one above it and the whole spec: aware of the other parts, not deep in them, so intent doesn't drift from hop to hop.

A ticket is right-sized when it fits one agent, about 100k tokens of context. Work can be too big for one agent, and it can be too small. Cut along what one agent understands together: work that needs the same thing understood belongs to one agent, who understands it once and uses it throughout. Spread it across agents, and each must understand the same thing the same way while working only one piece, which turns the work into a coordination problem. At that size you do the work yourself: you hold a leaf. A leaf may still carry children, such as three shoulds fixed under one implementer's ticket, and you may hill-climb to its requirements with native helpers, which never get tickets. A bigger ticket splits into sub-tickets, and their holders do the same until every part is a leaf.

Every agent dispatched onto a ticket is its captain, whatever the ticket: a change, a review, a fix. You choose how to reach its outcome: do the work yourself, orchestrating a little through native helpers, or split it and only orchestrate. A reviewer may run its review in two lanes, one helper driving the app while another reads the code, and it still owns the verdict. Once you split, you dispatch the writers and the reviewers, keep everything moving efficiently, and decide only the obvious; any other call becomes a ticket of its own.

Each ticket is one thing, done exceptionally: the writer's is the change, the reviewer's is the verdict, the splitting captain's is the whole. If each agent does its part perfectly, the swarm is unstoppable.
